# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Life Science Ecosystem Lab is a public research-software project. Participation is expected to be respectful, constructive, and attentive to the difference between engineering evidence, research interpretation, and scientific claims. Contributors should be able to question methods, identify errors, and disagree without personal hostility.

## Expected behavior

- Discuss ideas, methods, evidence, and code rather than attacking people
- Provide specific, reviewable reasoning when challenging a claim or implementation
- Acknowledge uncertainty, limitations, negative results, and corrections
- Respect different backgrounds, experience levels, and communication styles
- Keep public discussions relevant to the repository and its stated scope
- Protect privacy and do not disclose personal, health, patient, participant, or otherwise sensitive information

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, threats, or personal attacks
- Deliberate publication of private information, credentials, or access-controlled material
- Repeated disruptive conduct, bad-faith misrepresentation, or targeted abuse
- Presenting an unverified claim as established fact after its evidence boundary has been clarified

## Scope and enforcement

This policy applies to repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and other spaces where a person is representing or participating in this project. Maintainers may edit or remove contributions, decline proposed changes, or restrict participation when necessary to protect the community and the integrity of the project. Responses should be proportionate to the conduct and the available evidence.

To raise a conduct concern, contact the repository maintainer privately through GitHub rather than posting personal details in a public issue. Security vulnerabilities and sensitive disclosures must follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), not this public reporting route.

## Project-specific discussion principle

Technical disagreement is welcome. A passing test is not scientific validation, a source citation is not automatic claim support, and a correction is not a personal attack. Preserve these distinctions while treating contributors with respect.
